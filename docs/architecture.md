# Architecture notes

The project separates browser presentation, rendering, simulation, and server process without creating placeholders for deferred game systems.

## Current boundaries

- `client/src/engine` owns WebGPU-first renderer selection and the WebGL 2 fallback.
- `client/src/ui` mounts the canvas, reports renderer state, and presents the instrumentation HUD. Babylon owns the 3D scene and render loop.
- `client/src/game` connects keyboard intent, fixed-step simulation, rendering, quality settings, and HUD telemetry.
- `client/src/ships` turns interpolated simulation state into a procedural Arleigh Burke–class Flight IIA destroyer.
- `client/src/world` owns the shader ocean, atmospheric sky and lighting, wake geometry, animated seabirds and spatial ambience.
- `shared/src/simulation` contains renderer-independent boat motion and visual cruise-missile flight. Boat motion models thrust, drag, lateral damping, rudder lift, yaw inertia, distributed buoyancy, pitch, and roll without depending on Babylon.js.
- `shared/src/oceanWaves.ts` defines the analytical wave field used by both shared buoyancy queries and the client water shader.
- `client/src/physics` remains the Havok initialization boundary for future general collisions. The prototype's ship motion stays purpose-built rather than being forced into a generic rigid body.
- `server/src` is an independent Node.js process. Its health endpoint proves the process can start; there is no multiplayer protocol or game authority yet.

## Simulation and rendering flow

The client samples W/S/A/D (or arrow keys) into normalized throttle and steering intent. `BoatSimulation` advances at a fixed 60 Hz and keeps previous/current state for interpolation. It preserves world-space momentum during turns; lateral drag brings the velocity around to the new heading. Stern rudder lift scales with signed forward speed squared, so a stopped ship cannot pivot and astern steering reverses. Propulsion, rudder travel, and yaw have separate slow response times. The renderer consumes that state to move the boat, wake, ocean origin, smoothed camera target, and distant ambience. Because wave parameters are shared, the hull probes sample the same moving wave field displayed by the shader.

Simulation state uses meters, seconds, radians, and kilograms, with +Y up and +Z as the boat's forward axis. Future ship classes can share the intent/state boundary while providing different force, hull-probe, and steering configuration. This keeps render objects out of server simulation and leaves room to specialize patrol boats, larger surface ships, and submarines.

## Missile sea trials

`CruiseMissileSimulation` in `shared/src/simulation` advances visual flight with a fixed 1/120-second force-integration step and carries fractional elapsed time between updates. It exposes position, velocity, nose direction, wing deployment, booster state and boost/turn/cruise/expired phases without creating render objects. Momentum changes continuously during the vertical rise, curved pitch-over, stage transition and altitude capture. The client snapshots the chosen cell's ship-root launch point and deck normal through the current ship world transform, and adds the moving boat's world-space velocity at ignition. Each flight expires within 30 seconds; force coefficients, steering limits, stage timings and altitudes are artistic game values.

`destroyerVls` creates independently hinged hatch panels and their attached seams, tabs and grips. The destroyer exposes 32 forward and 64 aft cells, with the rear bank on the hangar roof near the middle deck. Six shared thin-instance meshes draw the 288 hatch parts; changing a hinge updates its instance matrices, with conservative bounds covering the full opening sweep. These hosts are excluded from static material merging. `MissileVls` owns the local demonstration ammunition counters, a 3.5-second gameplay interval, hatch sequencing, missile meshes, folding wings, flame lighting and detached booster debris. R and T request forward and rear launches through `GameSession`; HUD buttons use the same boundary.

`MissileVls` reuses bounded particle systems for deck exhaust, booster trails, thin cruise exhaust and turbulent fire. Emission positions are recorded in world space, leaving historical trails behind the vessel; wind, buoyancy, expansion and fade shape the billowing smoke. The dense rocket trail dissipates after booster cutoff; faint cruise exhaust is a visual cue. Quality settings scale emission density. Particle age and motion use the bounded game clock even after renderer stalls. Active missiles and debris have bounded lifetimes, expired meshes are disposed, and one shared launch light is disabled after nearby booster fire ends. Session teardown releases the light, shared particle systems, textures and materials. The optional Track launch camera consumes the active flight's render position and returns to the ship when it expires. These effects provide weapon sea-trial visuals while target engagement, damage and multiplayer authority remain deferred.

## Performance decisions

North Watch uses one shared analytical terrain and coastline in `shared/src/world/islandTerrain.ts`. An elongated, irregular outline encloses low exposed coastal cliffs and two broad eastern rocky highlands, with a deep southern bay open to navigation. The client builds a closed terrain mesh from that surface; the same outline drives renderer-independent, swept full-hull collision against land and the small lagoon pier footprints in `BoatSimulation`. Corrected motion feeds distance telemetry and interpolation. Terrain detail is deterministic, with instanced vegetation and material-batched base fittings. Both ocean shader backends share the coast shaping and lagoon dimensions for turquoise shallows and broken reef surf.

The island has a dedicated `IslandCamera` orbit camera. Camera transitions clear held input and switch attached pointer controls without changing ship state. In island mode, movement keys pan the base view while ship intent is neutral and weapon requests are blocked. The HUD follows the session's camera telemetry. Water geometry and sunlight focus on the active view, while the ocean's ship wake uniforms keep following the actual vessel. Both WebGL and WebGPU shaders render shallow water and shore foam along the terrain's radial coastline.

- The ocean is one continuously camera-centred square covering ±12,288 m. A dense central square and seven rings double cell spacing towards the horizon; shared vertices and split coarse edges stitch every boundary. Immutable buffers retain regular triangle shapes without per-frame rebuilds or snapped origin jumps. High/Medium/Low use 116,544 / 52,096 / 29,472 triangles, with 2 / 3 / 4 m central spacing and 16-bit indices.
- Six normalized directional wave components share wavelengths, amplitudes and phases between both shader backends and CPU queries. Frequencies follow deep-water dispersion, omega² = gk; a low-steepness second-order Stokes harmonic sharpens crests. Vertex shaders evaluate height and analytic slopes once per vertex, fading undersampled displacement using conservative cell sizes. Interpolated slopes avoid repeating six wave evaluations at every pixel. The water clock matches the hull's render interpolation.
- `sampleOceanSurface` fills a reusable height/slope/encounter-velocity result. Twenty-one weighted waterplane probes use precomputed hull moments; added heave mass, hydrostatic restoring forces and damping relative to water motion drive heave, pitch and roll. Probe encounter velocity includes translation and yaw. Buoyancy remains deterministic at the fixed 60 Hz step and server substeps. The coefficients and deep-water sea approximate fair-weather ship response; coastal surf and wake disturbance are visual approximations.
- A cached 256² periodic wind-sea texture contains 24 directional bands over a 32 m tile: RG stores slopes and B crest variation. It uploads once per ocean material and occupies roughly 350 KB including mipmaps. Hardware mip filtering removes subpixel detail; Low uses one lookup and Medium/High two decorrelated lookups. View-dependent seawater Fresnel and a filtered GGX sun highlight use the existing sky capture. Open-water fragments skip coastal noise, and hull foam shading runs only near moving bow/shoulder/stern regions.
- `BoatWake` shares the ocean vertex shader under `WAKE_SURFACE`; CPU buffers contain foam offsets and world-space history rather than sampled water heights. The GPU applies the same wave and ship disturbance functions each render. Wake ring filtering follows the active camera grid and quality spacing, while bow geometry and new emissions use the interpolated ship pose. Historical shape buffers update at 30 Hz; GPU height animation continues between uploads.
- High/Medium/Low adjust water ring resolution, slope texture lookups and detail strength, sky texture lookups, bird counts, shadow-map size, render scaling, and camera range. No recurring reflection/refraction pass, CPU water-mesh deformation or per-frame texture upload is introduced.
- `gullGeometry` builds shaped adult gull bodies, eyes, bills, tails, grey mantles and black-tipped feathered wings. `gullFlight` maintains two staggered, world-anchored passes with analytic velocity, coordinated bank, independent gliding and short wingbeat bursts. `AmbientSprites` retains its original integration name but now draws three shared thin-instance meshes, updating preallocated body/inner-wing/outer-wing matrices. Reusable position/velocity snapshots drive audio. Each pass anchors near the active camera's sightline once, then stays in world space as the camera moves.
- The blue daytime sky and water have native WGSL/GLSL shaders. The sky uses an inexpensive atmospheric scattering approximation, a 0.53-degree solar disc with pixel filtering, a forward-scattering aureole and a cached 512-pixel cumulus density/light texture. Low/Medium/High use one/two/three cloud samples; wind drift changes uniforms only. The sky renders after opaque sea/terrain at far depth, preserving depth and avoiding shading hidden sky pixels. Camera-relative directions and explicit current-pass eye binding support parented gun cameras and all probe faces. A 64-pixel cubemap captures only the initial sky/cloud field once for boat materials and sea reflections; reflection clouds approximate the slowly drifting live sky.
- Static destroyer details are merged by material; the rotating search radar and movable gun and VLS hatch parts retain their own transforms. Camera distances, shadow coverage, and the wake use the full-size 155 m hull scale.
- `MaritimeAudio` creates a small Web Audio graph on user interaction. Looping filtered noise supplies sea/wind, turbine and engine harmonics plus propeller noise track throttle/speed, and H triggers a two-tone ship whistle. Recorded CC0 herring-gull flight calls decode once, with a cached harmonic/raspy fallback. Two reusable filter/gain/HRTF voice chains follow eligible nearby birds, with inverse-distance fading, air filtering and bounded Doppler; sparse per-bird/global cadence suppresses calls when birds are absent or distant. Listener and sources mirror Z between Babylon's left-handed world and Web Audio. Camera jumps do not create extreme Doppler. First sea clicks or movement keys resume audio, while explicit mute persists. Status follows the AudioContext state. Sources are reused or disconnected after playback; the context is suspended when hidden and closed on disposal.
- Optional Babylon shader passes are registered before rendering, avoiding missing `.fx` requests during development. Quality changes retain WebGPU and WebGL parity.
- Weapon sea-trial visuals run locally in Phase 1; target engagement, persistence, and a multiplayer protocol remain deferred.

- The forward Mk 45 firing effect snapshots the muzzle position and barrel direction for each shot. World-space propellant puffs decelerate into a crosswind, expand and fade; individual particle systems own their textures and dispose after their particles expire. Conventional practice shells have no luminous trail. Recoil has a short kick and slower runout along the elevated barrel, with a 3.2-second reload interval.
- Gun audio prefetches a local, attributed public-domain USS Dewey Mk 45 report, decodes once after audio activation and reuses the buffer per discharge. Playback follows the existing master volume/mute and visibility state. Active gunshot sources are tracked and disconnected on completion or session disposal. A procedural report covers loading/decoding failure.

- `destroyerFlightDeck` owns the aft aviation surface and equipment. Its vertices sample the same hull beam and deck height functions as the main weather deck; UVs use physical deck coordinates so landing markings retain their proportions. The helicopter, tie-downs, track edges and lowered net frames are static and join the existing material batches. Alpha-tested safety-net weave retains open gaps without adding hundreds of wire draw calls.
- `createSeahawk` builds the secured, rotor-spread MH-60R-style aircraft separately from general ship fittings, following the supplied grey Indian Navy Seahawk references. The cockpit panes sit on the outer airframe surface, engine intakes have open lips, and rotor faces point outward. Landing wheels sample the flight-deck height. A symmetric pair of torpedo-like anti-submarine stores hangs from short side pylons. Airframe lofts, glazing, sensors, rotor hubs, landing gear, stores and chains remain static procedural geometry in the existing material batches.

- Hull geometry uses monotone Hermite interpolation of the dimension stations with ten points per transverse section. Hull fittings intersect the same generated upper-side triangles, so doors, drains, anchors and hull lettering remain seated after shape changes.
- `destroyerMast` owns the open tripod, two narrow signal yards, access galleries, sensor stack and rigging. Its single search-radar mesh is excluded from static batching and retains its rotating transform. `destroyerReferenceFittings` owns the detailed RHIBs, cradles, davits, boat-bay service gear and bridge sensors; custom RHIB hulls carry the same vertex attributes as the standard fitting meshes for batching.
- `destroyerSurface` generates matte naval paint and restrained aging. Hull UVs track longitudinal position and height in meters; the texture upload explicitly preserves canvas row orientation so the boot topping remains at the waterline. Upperworks share a low-contrast modulation texture. Ship-scale shadow offsets scale with map resolution to suppress self-shadow acne on broad painted steel faces.
